Old Android phone vs Raspberry Pi: which home server fits?

An old Android phone is a useful home server if you already own one and want media, files or small web apps. A Raspberry Pi is a better fit for GPIO, USB radios and control over the whole Linux system.

Hardware varies by phone. No blanket speed or power claim here. Raspberry Pi details: official Raspberry Pi 5 specifications.

What the phone already has

A screen for Dock mode, a camera for IPCam or a webcam, and Wi-Fi. Its battery can keep the phone running during a short power cut, but it will not power your router or an external drive. A worn battery is a maintenance problem, not a free UPS.

Where Android gets in the way

Homedroid runs without root. Linux apps use proot, which adds overhead to system calls. Apps cannot bind ports below 1024 or access GPIO, serial devices and USB Zigbee/Z-Wave radios. Home Assistant is for network and cloud integrations here.

Vendor battery savers and Android 12+ background process limits need one-time setup. USB storage depends on OTG support, the adapter and the filesystem. FAT32 files cannot exceed 4 GB.

Jellyfin and local AI depend on the phone

Jellyfin uses the phone’s MediaCodec encoder where supported; decoding, scaling and subtitle burn-in remain on the CPU. Small llama.cpp models run on 64-bit phones, with speed limited by memory, temperature and GPU drivers. There is no useful single “phone vs Pi” benchmark for all of that.

Measure your own phone with tools/bench.py and share the result with its model and Android version.
